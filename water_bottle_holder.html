Clip on Water Bottle Holder

The idea for this design came from a personal and ongoing issue that I kept running into, accidentally knocking over my water bottle in quiet settings like classrooms or at night, where the noise is both unnecessary and embarrassing. After it happened multiple times, it became clear that this was a small but common problem that could be easily avoided with a simple solution. My initial idea was to design a holder that attached off the side of a table; however, as I thought more about stability, ease of printing, and the need to support larger water bottles, this approach began to feel unreliable. Moving the design onto the tabletop allowed for better balance and overall support while still keeping the bottle secure and out of the way. This made the concept more practical and dependable, as it directly prevents bottles from tipping over in the first place. The sketches shown to the right reflect the different iterations and improvements made throughout this idea-development stage, leading into the more refined design process outlined below.
This project began with the idea of repurposing a tripod phone clip to function as a table-mounted water bottle holder. After finding that existing designs did not match my concept, I built a custom structure in Tinkercad by designing a rectangular connector to fit the phone clip, extending it with a perpendicular and parallel support arm, and attaching a hollow cylinder to hold the water bottle. Multiple design and printing issues occurred, including misalignment that caused parts of the model to print in midair, instability due to poor height placement, a cylinder that was too small to fit standard bottles, and a clip that could not support the weight of the full structure. These challenges required revisiting and adjusting the design multiple times. Ultimately, the limitations of precision and stability led to transitioning the project to Onshape, where the focus shifted to improving dimensions, weight distribution, and overall reliability.
In the Onshape phase of the project, I redesigned the model to improve precision, stability, and scalability. I began by creating a new table holder from scratch, intending to secure it to the table using a bolt-and-nut mechanism. Although this required multiple attempts, it allowed me to better understand tolerances and fastening challenges in 3D printing. I then redesigned the water bottle holder to be significantly larger so it could accommodate a variety of bottle sizes, while also increasing wall height to prevent tipping, even if the holder leaned against a wall. The connector was designed as a separate component to ensure accurate fitting between the table holder and the bottle holder, with added flexible clip features to allow minor adjustments without reprinting the entire model. The most challenging aspect was the bolt and nut design, which initially failed due to improper threading geometry. After seeking guidance from the maker lab and identifying the lack of a lead-in on the threads, I redesigned the fastener to improve functionality. Overall, the Onshape redesign addressed the limitations of the earlier model by improving fit, weight distribution, and modularity.

OnShape vs. Tinkercad

Overall, Tinkercad was much easier to use when it came to quickly designing simple components, such as the connector and cylindrical holder, and it allowed me to turn an idea into a model very fast. However, that simplicity also led to complications, especially with alignment and proportions, which resulted in issues like gaps, instability, and parts not sitting flat or standing properly. Onshape, while more challenging to learn at first, allowed for a higher level of precision and control, which helped eliminate these problems by making it easier to define exact dimensions and relationships between parts. This difference became especially clear when refining the design to ensure everything was properly aligned and supported. Based on this experience, I would recommend Tinkercad for quick, straightforward, and less complex designs, while Onshape is better suited for more detailed projects that require accuracy, structural planning, and long-term refinement.

Project Review

This project was challenging, but in a way that made the process engaging rather than frustrating. Working through design limitations and printing errors pushed me to problem-solve and think more critically about how digital designs translate into physical objects. It also gave me hands-on experience with new software, specifically Tinkercad and Onshape, which helped me better understand 3D modeling, precision, and design constraints. One of the most rewarding aspects of the project was seeing the idea evolve over time. What started as a rough sketch turned into a functional design through multiple iterations, redesigns, and improvements. Being able to physically test the design, identify what did not work, and then refine it made the project feel very real and meaningful. Overall, the experience not only strengthened my technical skills but also made the design process feel more approachable and enjoyable.

Rather than combining the water bottle holder and the connector into a single piece, I chose to separate them to improve both print reliability and functionality. Keeping the parts separate allowed me to increase the size of the water bottle holder, making it capable of supporting a wider range of bottle sizes without compromising stability. I began the design process by creating the connector and carefully matching its dimensions to the cutouts in the table holder. To reduce the risk of having to reprint large components if the fit was slightly off, I incorporated thin, clip-like features into the connector. These clips were designed to flex slightly during assembly, tightening the connection and allowing for small adjustments. As a result, any fit issues could be resolved by reprinting only the small clip components rather than the entire model, which helped conserve both time and material while improving the overall modularity of the design.
